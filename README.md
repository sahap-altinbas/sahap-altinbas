## Current Research Initiative

### KNES — Küresel Nükleer Etki Simülatörü

A scientific geospatial simulation initiative for modeling the physical and humanitarian consequences of nuclear detonations using traceable datasets, documented scientific models, explicit assumptions, validation procedures, and uncertainty quantification.

The project is being developed under a strict evidence and provenance framework:

- no undocumented model assumptions,
- no fabricated or unverifiable data,
- explicit source provenance,
- reproducible calculations,
- model-version traceability,
- uncertainty disclosure,
- separation of verified data, analytical inference, and scenario assumptions.

KNES is currently under controlled development.

## Research Principles

### Evidence over assertion

Analytical conclusions should be supported by traceable evidence rather than authority, intuition, or presentation.

### Reproducibility

Methods, transformations, assumptions, and model versions should be documented sufficiently to permit independent review.

### Provenance

Every critical dataset and scientific model should have an identifiable origin, version, licensing status, and validation record.

### Explicit uncertainty

Uncertainty is part of the analytical result and should not be concealed behind false precision.

### Analytical separation

Where relevant, assessments distinguish between:

1. directly verified information,
2. strongly supported analytical inference,
3. explicitly identified scenario assumptions or hypotheses.

### Security by design

Sensitive systems and research workflows should follow least-privilege access, controlled disclosure, and appropriate operational-security practices.
